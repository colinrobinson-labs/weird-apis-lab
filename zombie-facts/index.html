ZOMBIE FACTS

Facts that officially died but keep walking around. We pull fresh retractions from Crossref (which includes the Retraction Watch database), then check whether Wikipedia is still citing them. Every match is a zombie. v0.1: detection is live, outreach is a mock.

How detection works (and how reliable it is)
StepSourceReliability
1. Find retraction notices, newest firstCrossref REST API, filter=update-type:retraction. Notices carry update-to pointing at the original DOI.A. Publisher-registered, and Retraction Watch data merged into Crossref.
2. Look up the original paperCrossref /works/{doi}A
3. Is it still cited?Wikipedia search, insource:"DOI" across English WikipediaB. An exact DOI match is strong. A page may already say "retracted" next to it, which v0.2 will check before flagging.
4. News still citing itGDELT DOC API (planned v0.2)C. Fuzzy title match needs human review.

Rule: we never call the people citing it liars. A zombie is a citation that predates or ignores an official correction. The outreach message says exactly that.

Outreach loop (mock, needs a backend in v0.2)

Detected → Notified (polite note to the page talk/editor or newsroom corrections desk) → Responded → Fixed or Declined. Every step is public with timestamps. Draft notice:

Hi, a quick heads-up from Zombie Facts, a free public tracker of facts that were officially corrected.

Your page "{PAGE}" cites:
  {TITLE} ({DOI})
That paper was retracted by {PUBLISHER} on {DATE}. Notice: https://doi.org/{NOTICE}

No action is required. If you update or annotate the citation, reply and we'll mark it fixed publicly. If you think the retraction doesn't affect your use of it, tell us and we'll publish your response alongside.

Thanks for keeping things accurate.

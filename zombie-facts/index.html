How it works

  1. Retractions come from Crossref. A retraction notice links to the paper it kills, and we confirm that link live.
  2. Wikipedia is searched for the exact DOI in article source.
  3. For each hit we read the citation and the text around it. If it says “retracted” or uses Wikipedia's Retracted template, it's flagged. If not, it's a zombie.

What a zombie isn't

A zombie citation is not an accusation. Some articles cite a retracted paper on purpose, to discuss the retraction. Our check catches most of those as flagged, but a human should read the context before anyone edits. Every card links to the exact article and the official notice.

Notice we'd send

Hi, a note from Zombie Facts, a free public tracker of retracted research.

"{PAGE}" cites:
  {TITLE}
  https://doi.org/{DOI}
which was retracted ({NOTICE}).

If the citation is there on purpose, no action needed. Otherwise you may want to replace it or mark it with {{Retracted}}.

How it works

No opinions. Just the gap.

Every crisis here has a 2026 UN humanitarian appeal for a single country. For each one we measure two things and rank each against the others:

  1. Money. The share of the appeal that is still unfunded, from the UN Financial Tracking Service.
  2. Attention. English-language news articles in the last 30 days that mention the country alongside words like humanitarian, famine or displaced, counted by the GDELT Project, for every million people in need. This is averaged with Wikipedia reads of the country’s article per thousand people in need.

The Forgotten score is the average of those two ranks, from 0 to 100. Crises with fewer than 100,000 people in need are left out of the ranking so a tiny denominator can’t dominate.

What this misses: news in languages other than English, money that never gets reported to the UN, and crises with no UN appeal at all. Funding reports lag by weeks. People-in-need figures are the UN’s own planning numbers for the year shown.

How we pick where to give. An organisation only appears under a crisis if the UN recorded money reaching it there this year, and it is an established international group we list by name. We link straight to its own donation page. No middlemen, no affiliate links, no tracking, nothing we earn.
